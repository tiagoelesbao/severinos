## 📝 Caption / Descrição original

Markus Persson, better known as Notch, the creator of Minecraft, posted just two words on July 14: “Reject AI.” The post reached around 70,000 likes and 5.2 million views. Coming from one of gaming’s most respected programmers, it carried weight.

A week later, his tone started changing. He thanked people for tips on AI-assisted coding, admitted he would probably stay grumpy about it for a while, and asked who he was to resist the inevitable. Soon after, he was already imagining tools he could build with AI, including a text editor that could add new features to itself.

By July 22, he said he needed to give AI a real chance before deciding that being against it was worth more than being right. Two days later, he admitted he was running out of excuses and had come close to becoming a Luddite.

Then came the full shift. He started testing Claude, thinking up real use cases like converting an entire codebase from one language to another, and was soon praising the interface and how well it worked inside his editor.

From “Reject AI” to actually using it took just 14 days, and he documented the whole change of mind in public.
